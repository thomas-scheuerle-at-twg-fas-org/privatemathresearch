\title{A Six-Parameter Extension of the\\
Direct Somos--4 Algebraic Generating Function}
\author{Thomas Scheuerle}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In an earlier paper we gave a five-parameter quadratic algebraic generating
function whose Hankel determinants begin with
$(\lambda,m,r,\eta)$ and satisfy a normalized Somos--4 recurrence.  Here we
allow the coefficient of $H_{k+3}H_{k+1}$ to vary as well:
\[
 H_{k+4}H_k=\alpha H_{k+3}H_{k+1}+\tau H_{k+2}^2.
\]
The main construction is simple.  Introduce $q$ with $q^6=\alpha$, rescale the
five old parameters by suitable powers of $q$, and replace $x$ by $qx$.  This
produces the desired six-parameter Hankel sequence while leaving the prescribed
first four determinants unchanged.

There is, however, an important distinction between the determinant sequence
and the particular generating function used to produce it.  The determinant
sequence is rational in the six parameters, and so is a quartic model of the
associated algebraic curve.  The transported generating function is not: its
coefficient of $x$ is already $q^{-5}r$.  Thus the determinants and the curve can
be written without adjoining a root of $\alpha$, even though this particular
power-series representation cannot.

We compare this construction with the general quadratic-orthogonal-pair
construction of Chang and Liu.  After a direct shift, their bilateral Somos--4
sequence agrees with ours, and their subdiagonal parameters satisfy
$d_n=B_{n+2}$.  Their square-root choice $u^2=\alpha$ also has a concrete
meaning.  Replacing $u$ by $-u$ is exactly the same as replacing a generating
series $F(x)$ by $F(-x)$.  This leaves every ordinary Hankel determinant
unchanged, but multiplies the once-shifted determinant of order $N$ by
$(-1)^N$.  In this sense the ordinary Hankel sequence forgets a parity choice
that the shifted determinants still remember.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The preceding paper~\cite{Scheuerle2026} starts with four prescribed Hankel
determinants,
\[
 (H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)=(\lambda,m,r,\eta),
\]
and constructs an algebraic generating function for which
\begin{equation}
 H_{k+4}H_k=H_{k+3}H_{k+1}+\tau H_{k+2}^2,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:old-somos4}
\end{equation}
Here we ask what happens when the coefficient of the first product is no longer
fixed to $1$:
\begin{equation}
 H_{k+4}H_k=\alpha H_{k+3}H_{k+1}+\tau H_{k+2}^2,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:new-somos4}
\end{equation}
We therefore have six independent parameters
$(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$.  Setting $\alpha=1$ takes us back to the
construction of the preceding paper.

There are two questions, and it is useful not to mix them.  The first is an
existence question: can we extend the old construction so that the first four
Hankel determinants remain exactly $(\lambda,m,r,\eta)$ and the recurrence is
\eqref{eq:new-somos4}?  The answer is yes.  Section~2 gives an explicit weighted
rescaling.

The second question asks for more.  Can such a quadratic algebraic generating
function be written using only rational functions of the six parameters, that
is, over
\[
 K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau),
\]
while also keeping the shifted-determinant identity $D_N=H_{N+2}$?  Our
weighted construction does not do this.  It uses a sixth root $q$ of $\alpha$,
and its coefficient of $x$ is $q^{-5}r$.  This is not a cosmetic radical that
disappears after simplification.

A small vocabulary point will make the rest of the paper easier to read.  By
an \emph{ordinary Hankel sequence} we mean the determinants $H_N$ of a
coefficient sequence.  A \emph{once-shifted determinant} $D_N$ uses the same
coefficients with every index increased by one.  We sometimes call a chosen
coefficient sequence a \emph{moment sequence}, following the standard language
of continued fractions and orthogonal polynomials.  No analytic measure is
needed here: throughout this paper, ``moments'' are simply the coefficients of
the formal generating series.  Likewise, the \emph{Somos--4 orbit} means the
sequence obtained by iterating the recurrence once its initial values are fixed.

One theme of the paper is that the following three objects carry different
amounts of information:
\[
 \text{Hankel determinant sequence},\qquad
 \text{algebraic curve},\qquad
 \text{chosen generating series}.
\]
The first two can be written over $K$.  A particular generating series need not
be.  We will use \emph{descend to $K$} as shorthand for ``can be written with
coefficients in $K$''.  With this terminology, the determinant sequence and the
curve descend, while the particular transported series does not.

This distinction also explains our relation to recent work.  Liu, Wang, and
Zhang~\cite{LiuWangZhang2026} give broad rational-function-field constructions
for general $(\alpha,\beta)$--Somos--4 Hankel transforms.  Here we impose the
additional requirements that four initial determinants are independently
prescribed and that a specific shifted-determinant normalization is retained.
So our question is narrower.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~2 performs the weighted rescaling
and proves the six-parameter recurrence.  Section~3 tracks the shifted
determinants and shows exactly where the fractional power of $\alpha$ first
appears.  Section~4 shows that the determinant sequence and its curve can still
be written over $K$.  Section~5 translates the construction into the notation
of Chang and Liu and identifies the corresponding Jacobi subdiagonal data.
Section~6 explains the remaining sign choice as the elementary parity operation
$F(x)\mapsto F(-x)$.  Section~7 collects the main conclusions.

\section{From the five-parameter construction to six parameters}
\subsection{The five-parameter input}
Let
\[
 G_{1;\lambda,m,r,\eta,\tau}(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}g_kx^k
\]
be the selected algebraic series of \cite{Scheuerle2026}.  Define
\[
 H_N(g)=\det(g_{i+j})_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},\qquad
 D_N(g)=\det(g_{i+j+1})_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}.
\]
The normalization in the preceding paper is
\begin{equation}
 (H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)=(\lambda,m,r,\eta),
 \qquad D_N=H_{N+2}.
 \label{eq:old-normalization}
\end{equation}
Its quadratic discriminant factors as
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_1(x)
 =\bigl(x\lambda^2\eta m r^2\bigr)^2
  \left((1-Jx+\tau x^2)^2-4x^3\right),
 \label{eq:old-discriminant}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 J=\frac{\lambda\eta}{mr}
   +\frac{m^2}{\lambda r}
   +\frac{r^2}{m\eta}
   +\frac{\tau mr}{\lambda\eta}.
 \label{eq:old-J}
\end{equation}
We will use only these two facts from the five-parameter construction.

\subsection{Weighted transport}
Work over
\[
 \K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,q,\tau),\qquad q^6=\alpha,
\]
and introduce the primed parameters
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'=\lambda,\qquad m'=q^{-2}m,\qquad
 r'=q^{-6}r,\qquad \eta'=q^{-12}\eta,\qquad
 \tau'=q^{-8}\tau.
 \label{eq:parameter-scaling}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 G_{\alpha;\lambda,m,r,\eta,\tau}(x)
 :=G_{1;\lambda',m',r',\eta',\tau'}(qx).
 \label{eq:transported-G}
\end{equation}
If
\[
 G_{1;\lambda',m',r',\eta',\tau'}(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}a_kx^k,
 \qquad
 G_{\alpha;\lambda,m,r,\eta,\tau}(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}b_kx^k,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 b_k=q^ka_k.
 \label{eq:coefficient-scaling}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Determinant weights]
For every $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 H_N(b)=q^{N(N-1)}H_N(a),\qquad
 D_N(b)=q^{N^2}D_N(a).
 \label{eq:determinant-weights}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the ordinary Hankel matrix, its $(i,j)$ entry is multiplied by $q^{i+j}$.
Factoring $q^i$ from row $i$ and $q^j$ from column $j$ gives the exponent
$2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}j=N(N-1)$.  For the shifted matrix the entry is multiplied
by $q^{i+j+1}$.  The additional factor $q$ in every row contributes $N$, so
the total exponent is $N(N-1)+N=N^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The six-parameter Somos--4 recurrence}
\begin{theorem}
Let $\Ha_N=H_N(b)$.  Then
\[
 (\Ha_1,\Ha_2,\Ha_3,\Ha_4)=(\lambda,m,r,\eta)
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 \Ha_{k+4}\Ha_k
 =\alpha\Ha_{k+3}\Ha_{k+1}+\tau\Ha_{k+2}^2,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:transported-recurrence}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:determinant-weights} and
\eqref{eq:parameter-scaling} give
\[
 \Ha_1=\lambda,
 \quad \Ha_2=q^2m'=m,
 \quad \Ha_3=q^6r'=r,
 \quad \Ha_4=q^{12}\eta'=\eta.
\]
Write $H'_N=H_N(a)$.  The primed sequence satisfies
\[
 H'_{k+4}H'_k=H'_{k+3}H'_{k+1}+\tau'H'_{k+2}{}^2.
\]
The weight difference between the left-hand product and the first product on
the right is $6$, while that between the left-hand product and
$H'_{k+2}{}^2$ is $8$.  Hence
\[
 \Ha_{k+4}\Ha_k
 =q^6\Ha_{k+3}\Ha_{k+1}+q^8\tau'\Ha_{k+2}^2.
\]
Now use $q^6=\alpha$ and $q^8\tau'=\tau$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Low-order recurrence check]
The first two values forced beyond the initial tuple are
\[
 \Ha_5=\frac{\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2}{\lambda},
\qquad
 \Ha_6=\frac{\alpha\Ha_5r+\tau\eta^2}{m}.
\]
These formulas provide a direct indexing check for any coefficient expansion
of \eqref{eq:transported-G}.
\end{corollary}

\section{Why fractional powers appear}
The rescaling above gives exactly the ordinary Hankel determinants we want.  The shifted determinants scale differently, however.  We compute that difference next; it is the source of the stronger rationality question.

\subsection{The transported companion identity}
\begin{proposition}
If the primed series obeys $D_N(a)=H'_{N+2}$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Da_N:=D_N(b)=q^{-3N-2}\Ha_{N+2},
 \qquad N\geq1.
 \label{eq:graded-companion}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:determinant-weights},
\[
 D_N(b)=q^{N^2}H'_{N+2},\qquad
 \Ha_{N+2}=q^{(N+2)(N+1)}H'_{N+2}.
\]
Their exponent difference is
$N^2-(N+2)(N+1)=-3N-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A constant coefficient rescaling $b_k\mapsto cb_k$ changes the ratio
$D_N(b)/\Ha_{N+2}$ by $c^{-2}$.  It cannot remove the factor $q^{-3N}$.
Thus the literal identity $D_N=H_{N+2}$ is not invariant under this graded
transport.  Equation \eqref{eq:graded-companion}, rather than the ungraded
identity, is the companion normalization naturally inherited from the
five-parameter series.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Parity conjugation and the companion orientation}
For any formal series
\[
 F(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}f_nx^n,
 \qquad
 P(F)(x):=F(-x)=\sum_{n\geq0}(-1)^nf_nx^n,
\]
the involution $P$ has different effects on the ordinary and once-shifted
Hankel determinants.

\begin{proposition}[Parity determinant laws]
For every $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 H_N(P(F))=H_N(F),\qquad
 D_N(P(F))=(-1)^N D_N(F).
 \label{eq:parity-determinant-laws}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $(i,j)$ entry of the ordinary Hankel matrix is multiplied by
$(-1)^{i+j}$.  Factoring $(-1)^i$ from row $i$ and $(-1)^j$ from column $j$
gives the total exponent
$2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}j=N(N-1)$, which is even.  In the once-shifted matrix the
entry is multiplied by $(-1)^{i+j+1}$.  The additional factor $-1$ in every
row contributes $(-1)^N$, proving the second identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Parity-conjugate companion law]
If $D_N(F)=c_NH_{N+2}(F)$, then
\begin{equation}
 D_N(P(F))=(-1)^Nc_NH_{N+2}(P(F)).
 \label{eq:parity-companion-law}
\end{equation}
In particular, the ordinary Hankel orbit cannot distinguish $F(x)$ from
$F(-x)$, whereas a prescribed companion law distinguishes the two parity
orientations whenever some odd-order companion determinant is nonzero.
\end{corollary}

Applied to the transported series, parity conjugation preserves the
six-parameter Somos--4 orbit and changes the graded companion identity to
\begin{equation}
 D_N(G_\alpha(-x))=(-1)^Nq^{-3N-2}H_{N+2}(G_\alpha(-x)).
 \label{eq:transported-parity-companion}
\end{equation}
So the effect is quite concrete: $F(x)$ and $F(-x)$ have the same ordinary Hankel determinants, but their shifted determinants differ by a predictable sign.  We use the word ``orientation only for this sign choice.  Nothing here requires an interpretation in terms of algebraic sheets or reversal of a bilateral Somos orbit.

\subsection{The first coefficients and failure of descent}
For the five-parameter selected series, the companion normalization gives
$g_0=\lambda$ and $g_1=r$.  Applied to the primed parameters in
\eqref{eq:parameter-scaling}, this yields $a_0=\lambda$ and
$a_1=r'=q^{-6}r$.  Therefore \eqref{eq:coefficient-scaling} gives
\begin{equation}
 b_0=\lambda,\qquad b_1=q^{-5}r=\frac{qr}{\alpha}.
 \label{eq:first-coefficients}
\end{equation}
The next two coefficients obtained by direct expansion are
\begin{align}
 b_2&=\frac{m}{\lambda}+\frac{q^{-10}r^2}{\lambda}
     =\frac{m}{\lambda}+\frac{q^2r^2}{\alpha^2\lambda},
 \label{eq:b2}\\
 b_3&=\frac{q^5m^2}{\lambda^2r}
 +\frac{q^3\eta}{\alpha r}
 +\frac{2qmr}{\alpha\lambda^2}
 +\frac{q^3r^3}{\alpha^3\lambda^2}.
 \label{eq:b3}
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G_\alpha(x)={}&\lambda+\frac{qr}{\alpha}x
 +\left(\frac{m}{\lambda}+\frac{q^2r^2}{\alpha^2\lambda}\right)x^2\\
&+\left(\frac{q^5m^2}{\lambda^2r}
 +\frac{q^3\eta}{\alpha r}
 +\frac{2qmr}{\alpha\lambda^2}
 +\frac{q^3r^3}{\alpha^3\lambda^2}\right)x^3+O(x^4).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:series-expansion}
\end{equation}
The first coefficient also follows from \eqref{eq:graded-companion} at $N=1$:
\[
 b_1=D_1(b)=q^{-5}H_3(b)=q^{-5}r.
\]
Thus the fractional power is not an artifact of a badly simplified formula.  It is already forced by the shifted-determinant relation at $N=1$.

Put
\[
 K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau),\qquad
 L=K(q),\qquad q^6=\alpha.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Non-descent of the transported branch]
Regard $\alpha$ as an indeterminate.  In the generic nondegenerate case
$r\neq0$, the transported series \eqref{eq:transported-G} does not belong to
$K[[x]]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Its coefficient of $x$ is $b_1=q^{-5}r=qr/\alpha$. The polynomial
$X^6-\alpha$ is irreducible over $K$ by Eisenstein's criterion at the prime
$\alpha$ in the corresponding polynomial ring. Hence $q\notin K$. Since
$r\neq0$ generically, $b_1\notin K$, and the transported series is not in
$K[[x]]$.
\end{proof}

Although the coefficients do not descend, the fractional powers cancel in the
ordinary Hankel determinants.  For example,
\[
 H_2(b)=b_0b_2-b_1^2
 =\lambda\left(\frac{m}{\lambda}
 +\frac{q^{-10}r^2}{\lambda}\right)-q^{-10}r^2=m.
\]
This example shows the distinction directly.  The radicals cancel from the ordinary Hankel determinants even though they remain in the coefficients of the transported series.

\section{What is defined over the six-parameter field}
The failure just proved concerns one particular generating series.  It does not mean that the recurrence itself, or its algebraic curve, needs fractional powers of $\alpha$.  We now check these two objects separately.

\subsection{Transport of the discriminant}
Separate the old invariant into
\begin{equation}
 A=\frac{\lambda\eta}{mr}+\frac{\tau mr}{\lambda\eta},
 \qquad
 B=\frac{m^2}{\lambda r}+\frac{r^2}{m\eta}.
 \label{eq:A-B}
\end{equation}
After substitution of \eqref{eq:parameter-scaling},
\begin{equation}
 J'=q^{-4}A+q^2B.
 \label{eq:J-prime}
\end{equation}
Consequently the substitution $x\mapsto qx$ gives
\begin{equation}
 qJ'=q^{-3}A+q^3B
 =\alpha^{-1/2}A+\alpha^{1/2}B.
 \label{eq:J-alpha}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Transported discriminant]
The discriminant obtained by substituting
\eqref{eq:parameter-scaling} and $x\mapsto qx$ into
\eqref{eq:old-discriminant} is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\alpha(x)
 =q^{-50}\bigl(x\lambda^2\eta m r^2\bigr)^2
 \left[
  \left(1-(q^{-3}A+q^3B)x+q^{-6}\tau x^2\right)^2
  -4q^3x^3
 \right].
 \label{eq:q-discriminant}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\alpha(x)
 =\alpha^{-25/3}\bigl(x\lambda^2\eta m r^2\bigr)^2
 \left[
  \left(1-(\alpha^{-1/2}A+\alpha^{1/2}B)x
             +\frac{\tau}{\alpha}x^2\right)^2
  -4\alpha^{1/2}x^3
 \right].
 \label{eq:alpha-discriminant}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The square prefactor has total $q$-weight
$2(1-12-2-12)=-50$.  The three terms in the residual factor transform as
$J'x\mapsto qJ'x$, $\tau'x^2\mapsto q^2\tau'x^2=q^{-6}\tau x^2$, and
$x^3\mapsto q^3x^3$.  Equations \eqref{eq:J-prime} and $q^6=\alpha$ give the
stated forms.
\end{proof}

The overall monomial factor in \eqref{eq:q-discriminant} is a square in
$\K(x)$ and does not affect the residual double cover.  It should, however,
be retained when comparing discriminants of particular polynomial
representatives, because multiplying a quadratic equation by a scalar squares
that scalar in its discriminant.

\subsection{A rational model of the curve}
Remove the square prefactor in \eqref{eq:alpha-discriminant} and put
\begin{equation}
 x=\alpha^{1/2}z.
 \label{eq:x-z}
\end{equation}
The residual equation becomes
\begin{equation}
 Y^2=\left(1-(A+\alpha B)z+\tau z^2\right)^2
      -4\alpha^2z^3.
 \label{eq:rational-quartic-z}
\end{equation}
All coefficients of \eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} belong to
$\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$.

For comparison with the quartic convention used in \cite{Scheuerle2026}, set
$X=z^{-1}$ and $y=z^{-2}Y$ (equivalently, $Y=z^2y$).  Then \eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} is birational
to
\begin{equation}
 y^2=\left(X^2-(A+\alpha B)X+\tau\right)^2-4\alpha^2X.
 \label{eq:rational-quartic-X}
\end{equation}
Thus the curve itself has coefficients in the rational six-parameter field.  But the change of variable \eqref{eq:x-z} also rescales the coefficients of a power series, so it does not preserve our normalized Hankel data.  Removing radicals from the curve is therefore not the same as removing them from the chosen generating series.

\subsection{Formal representatives with the same Hankel determinants}
The ordinary Hankel determinants do not determine a unique generating series.  This is worth making explicit.  For a nondegenerate Hankel sequence define
\begin{equation}
 \beta_n=\frac{H_{n-1}H_{n+1}}{H_n^2},\qquad H_0=1.
 \label{eq:jacobi-beta}
\end{equation}
All $H_n$ generated by \eqref{eq:transported-recurrence}, and hence all
$\beta_n$, lie in $K$.  For arbitrary $a_0,a_1,\ldots\in K$, the Jacobi
fraction
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde G(x)=
 \cfrac{H_1}{1-a_0x-
  \cfrac{\beta_1x^2}{1-a_1x-
   \cfrac{\beta_2x^2}{1-a_2x-\ddots}}}
 \label{eq:jacobi-representative}
\end{equation}
has ordinary Hankel determinants $H_n$.  So there are formal power series over $K$ with exactly the same ordinary Hankel determinants.  What we do \emph{not} get for free is the extra structure we care about: such a series need not be quadratic algebraic, need not satisfy the graded shifted-determinant identity, and need not be the distinguished Stieltjes choice.

\section{Comparison with the construction of Chang and Liu}
We have shown only that the transported quadratic series contains fractional powers.  A different quadratic series might conceivably avoid them.  Chang and Liu provide a natural comparison because their construction realizes a generic bilateral Somos--4 sequence by Hankel determinants.  We now put their formulas and ours into the same notation.

\subsection{The shifted Somos--4 orbit}
Chang and Liu \cite[Sections~3.3--4.2]{ChangLiu2026} treat the generic bilateral Somos--4 initial-value problem by Hankel determinants of quadratic orthogonal pairs. Their normalized recurrence is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{n+2}\sigma_{n-2}=a\sigma_{n+1}\sigma_{n-1}+b\sigma_n^2,
 \qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
 \label{eq:CL-recurrence}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{-1}=x$, $\sigma_0=1$, $\sigma_1=y$, and $\sigma_2=z$.
Our freely prescribed determinants fit this normalization after the shift
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_n=\frac{H_{n+2}}m,
 \qquad
 (x,y,z;a,b)=\left(\frac\lambda m,\frac r m,\frac\eta m;\alpha,\tau\right).
 \label{eq:CL-dictionary}
\end{equation}
This shift uses all six parameters.  In particular, it avoids the unwanted constraint
$\eta=\alpha\lambda r+\tau m^2$ that would follow from imposing our recurrence at $N=0$ with $H_0=1$.

Write $u^2=\alpha$ and define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal N&=\tau r^2m+\lambda^2\eta^2+\alpha\eta m^3+\alpha\lambda r^3,
 \label{eq:CL-Nplus}\\
 \mathcal N_0&=\tau r^2m-\lambda^2\eta^2+\alpha\eta m^3+\alpha\lambda r^3.
 \label{eq:CL-Nminus}
\end{align}
Under \eqref{eq:CL-dictionary}, the Chang--Liu parameters include
\begin{align}
 d_0&=\frac{\lambda r}{m^2},&u_0&=\frac{2r}{m},\\
 \frac vu&=\frac{\mathcal N}{2\alpha\lambda r\eta m},&
 uv&=\frac{\mathcal N}{2\lambda r\eta m},\\
 \frac{v_0}{u}&=\frac{\mathcal N_0}{2\alpha\lambda r\eta m},&
 uv_0&=\frac{\mathcal N_0}{2\lambda r\eta m},
 \label{eq:CL-parameters}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 f=\frac{\tau}{\alpha}-
 \frac{\mathcal N^2}{4\alpha\lambda^2r^2\eta^2m^2}.
 \label{eq:CL-f}
\end{equation}
The sign difference between $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal N_0$ is essential because $v_0$ enters the distinguished moment branch.
The scaling $X=uZ$ turns their curve into
\begin{equation}
 Y^2=(\alpha Z^2+f)^2+4\alpha Z-4uv,
 \label{eq:CL-descended-curve}
\end{equation}
which is defined over $K$. Again, this statement concerns the curve only.  It says nothing yet about whether the coefficients of a chosen generating series lie in $K$.

\subsection{Translation to Jacobi data}
Use the local Jacobi convention
\begin{equation}
 F(t)=\cfrac{\lambda}{1-A_0t-
 \cfrac{B_1t^2}{1-A_1t-
 \cfrac{B_2t^2}{1-A_2t-
 \cfrac{B_3t^2}{1-A_3t-B_4t^2M_4(t)}}}},
 \qquad M_4(0)=1.
 \label{eq:local-jacobi-convention}
\end{equation}
The product formula
\begin{equation}
 H_N=\lambda^N\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}B_j^{N-j}
 \label{eq:J-Hankel-product}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{align}
 B_1&=\frac{m}{\lambda^2},&
 B_2&=\frac{\lambda r}{m^2},&
 B_3&=\frac{m\eta}{r^2},&
 B_4&=\frac{r(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)}{\lambda\eta^2}.
 \label{eq:first-B}
\end{align}
The companion identities through $D_3=H_5$ force
\begin{align}
 A_0&=\frac r\lambda,&
 A_1&=\frac{\eta\lambda^2+m^2}{\lambda mr},&
 A_2&=\frac{\alpha\eta m^3+\lambda r^3+\tau m^2r^2}{\eta\lambda mr}.
 \label{eq:first-A}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Fourth shifted determinant]
For the convention \eqref{eq:local-jacobi-convention},
\begin{equation}
 D_4=\lambda^4B_1^3B_2^2B_3
 \left(A_0A_1A_2A_3-A_0A_1B_3-A_0A_3B_2-A_2A_3B_1+B_1B_3\right).
 \label{eq:generic-D4}
\end{equation}
After substitution of \eqref{eq:first-B} and \eqref{eq:first-A}, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
 D_4=\frac{\eta}{\lambda r^2}
 \left[A_3r(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)-\eta^2\lambda\right].
 \label{eq:D4-reduced}
\end{equation}
Consequently $D_4=H_6$, where
\[
 H_6=\frac{\alpha^2\eta mr+\alpha\tau r^3+\tau\eta^2\lambda}{\lambda m},
\]
uniquely forces
\begin{equation}
 A_3=
 \frac{\alpha^2\eta mr^3+\alpha\tau r^5+\eta^3\lambda m+\tau\eta^2\lambda r^2}
 {\eta mr(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)}.
 \label{eq:A3}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:generic-D4} follows by direct expansion of the shifted $4\times4$ Hankel determinant in Jacobi parameters.  Substitution and collection of the terms linear in $A_3$ gives \eqref{eq:D4-reduced}.  Solving \eqref{eq:D4-reduced} for $A_3$ after inserting the displayed value of $H_6$ gives \eqref{eq:A3}.
\end{proof}
An equivalent useful form is
\[
 A_3=\frac{\alpha r^2}{\eta m}+
 \frac{\eta\lambda(\eta m+\tau r^2)}{mr(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)}.
\]
No $B_4$ occurs in $D_4$: the fourth ordinary Hankel step determines $B_4$, while the fourth shifted step determines $A_3$, without imposing any extra relation among the six parameters.

Chang and Liu use
\[
 d_n=\frac{\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n+1}}{\sigma_n^2}.
\]
Substitution of \eqref{eq:CL-dictionary} gives the exact all-order interface
\begin{equation}
 d_n=\frac{H_{n+1}H_{n+3}}{H_{n+2}^2}=B_{n+2}.
 \label{eq:d-B}
\end{equation}
Thus $d_0=B_2$, $d_1=B_3$, and $d_2=B_4$.  Thus the subdiagonal parameters match after an index shift: only $B_1$ comes before the Chang--Liu sequence begins.  The diagonal parameters and the normalization of the generating-series coefficients still have to be treated separately.

\section{The parity involution in the Chang--Liu branch}
The Chang--Liu construction contains the same elementary parity symmetry, although it appears there as a choice of square root.  Let
\[
 S(t;u)=\sum_{n\geq0}s_n(u)t^n,\qquad u^2=\alpha,
\]
be their distinguished moment branch.  Chang and Liu, Remark~4.7, give
\begin{equation}
 S(t;-u)=S(-t;u),\qquad s_n(-u)=(-1)^ns_n(u).
 \label{eq:galois-parity}
\end{equation}
Thus the parameter involution $u\mapsto-u$ is exactly the moment-parity
involution of Proposition~\ref{eq:parity-determinant-laws}.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 H_N(S(t;-u))=H_N(S(t;u)),\qquad
 D_N(S(t;-u))=(-1)^ND_N(S(t;u)).
 \label{eq:CL-parity-determinants}
\end{equation}
So the two choices of $u$ do not produce two different ordinary Somos--4 sequences.  They produce coefficient sequences related by alternating signs.  Ordinary Hankel determinants cannot see this change; once-shifted determinants can.

We can now see the same fact directly in the coefficients.  Writing
\[
 S(t;u)=E(t^2)+utR(t^2),
\]
the relation \eqref{eq:galois-parity} implies, coefficientwise, that the even
moments are invariant under $u\mapsto-u$ and the odd moments are anti-invariant.
The appearance of $u=\sqrt\alpha$ in this selected branch therefore records the
parity orientation of its odd moments.  This observation concerns the selected
moment lift.  It does not assert that every representation of the ordinary
Hankel orbit requires a quadratic extension.

The shifted-determinant normalization tells the two choices apart.  If one branch
satisfies $D_N=c_NH_{N+2}$, then its parity conjugate satisfies
$D_N=(-1)^Nc_NH_{N+2}$.  Hence choosing a literal or graded companion law selects
one of the two parity orientations.  This is all we need from the involution.

\section{Summary}
The main facts can now be stated compactly.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The weighted transport \eqref{eq:transported-G} produces a quadratic
algebraic series over $K(q)$, $q^6=\alpha$, whose first four ordinary Hankel
determinants are $(\lambda,m,r,\eta)$ and whose complete ordinary Hankel orbit
satisfies \eqref{eq:transported-recurrence}.
\item Its once-shifted determinants satisfy the exact graded companion law
\eqref{eq:graded-companion}.  The coefficient $b_1=q^{-5}r$ proves generic
non-descent of this selected transported branch to $K[[x]]$.
\item The associated residual curve has the rational model
\eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} over $K$.  Thus descent of the orbit and the curve
does not imply descent of the selected moment branch.
\item The dictionary \eqref{eq:CL-dictionary} identifies the normalized
Chang--Liu orbit with the prescribed six-parameter orbit, and
\eqref{eq:d-B} gives the all-order subdiagonal interface $d_n=B_{n+2}$.
\item The involution $u\mapsto-u$ in the Chang--Liu moment branch is precisely
moment parity.  It fixes every ordinary Hankel determinant and multiplies the
once-shifted determinant of order $N$ by $(-1)^N$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
There is a simple construction and a simple warning.

The construction is the weighted substitution \eqref{eq:transported-G}.  With
$q^6=\alpha$, it extends the five-parameter generating function to the full
six-parameter Somos--4 recurrence.  Its first four ordinary Hankel determinants
remain $(\lambda,m,r,\eta)$, and the whole determinant sequence satisfies
\eqref{eq:transported-recurrence}.

The warning is that the coefficients of the generating series contain more
information than its ordinary Hankel determinants.  Under the same rescaling,
the shifted determinants obey \eqref{eq:graded-companion}, and already
$b_1=q^{-5}r$ lies outside
$K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$ in the generic case.  Nevertheless the
ordinary Hankel sequence lies in $K$, and the quartic
\eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} shows that the associated curve does too.  Thus
three superficially similar rationality questions have different answers: the
determinant sequence and the curve are rational in the six parameters, while
this particular generating series is not.

The comparison with Chang and Liu makes the remaining square-root choice easy
to interpret.  Their identity $S(t;-u)=S(-t;u)$ says that changing the sign of
$u=\sqrt\alpha$ merely alternates the signs of the odd coefficients.  This
leaves every ordinary Hankel determinant fixed and changes a once-shifted
determinant of order $N$ by $(-1)^N$.  In other words, ordinary Hankel
determinants forget this parity choice, while shifted determinants remember it.
No stronger geometric interpretation is required for the results proved here.
