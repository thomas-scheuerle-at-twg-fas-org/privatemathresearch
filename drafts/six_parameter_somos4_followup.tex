\begin{abstract}
The preceding paper constructed a five-parameter quadratic algebraic generating
function whose first four Hankel determinants are
$(\lambda,m,r,\eta)$ and whose Hankel sequence satisfies a normalized
Somos--4 recurrence with coefficient $\tau$.  We ask whether the same method
extends to the general two-coefficient recurrence
\[
 H_{k+4}H_k=\alpha H_{k+3}H_{k+1}+\tau H_{k+2}^2.
\]
A weighted transport with $q^6=\alpha$ gives an exact six-parameter
construction and preserves the prescribed ordinary Hankel determinants.
However, its selected generating-function branch contains fractional powers of
$\alpha$; already its first nonconstant coefficient is $q^{-5}r$.  This leads
to the sharper question whether another quadratic-algebraic representative can
be defined over the rational parameter field while retaining the companion
normalization $D_N=H_{N+2}$.

We show that the difficulty belongs neither to the Somos--4 orbit nor to its
associated genus-one curve.  The Hankel sequence is defined over the rational
six-parameter field, and the transported discriminant has the rational quartic
model
\[
 Y^2=\bigl(1-(A+\alpha B)z+\tau z^2\bigr)^2-4\alpha^2z^3.
\]
We then compare the construction with the generic quadratic-orthogonal-pair
realization of Chang and Liu.  An explicit shift identifies their bilateral
orbit with ours, and their subdiagonal data satisfy $d_n=B_{n+2}$.  The
comparison also separates three notions that need not descend together: the
Hankel sequence, the algebraic curve, and a chosen moment branch.  The first
two descend, whereas the distinguished transported and Chang--Liu branches do
not descend generically.  We rule out several natural repairs, including
parity rescaling, geometric scaling, and injective finite prefixes over the
base field.  A genuinely non-diagonal construction from the invariant even
and odd parts remains possible, so no general nonexistence claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
The purpose of this paper is to extend the five-parameter generating function
of \cite{Scheuerle2026} and to understand a difficulty that becomes visible in
the extension.  The previous construction prescribes
\[
 (H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)=(\lambda,m,r,\eta)
\]
and produces the recurrence
\begin{equation}
 H_{k+4}H_k=H_{k+3}H_{k+1}+\tau H_{k+2}^2,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:old-somos4}
\end{equation}
The natural generalization is to free the coefficient of the first bilinear
term:
\begin{equation}
 H_{k+4}H_k=\alpha H_{k+3}H_{k+1}+\tau H_{k+2}^2,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:new-somos4}
\end{equation}
The six independent parameters are therefore
$(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$, and $\alpha=1$ recovers the normalization of
the preceding paper.

Two questions guide the discussion.  First, can the earlier algebraic
construction be extended to arbitrary values of these six parameters while
preserving the four prescribed Hankel determinants?  We answer this question
affirmatively by a weighted change of parameters and variable.  Second, can
one obtain such a generating function directly over
\[
 K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)
\]
without fractional powers of $\alpha$, while also retaining the ungraded
companion identity $D_N=H_{N+2}$?  The weighted construction does not answer
the second question: it naturally lives over a field containing a sixth root
of $\alpha$, and its first nonconstant coefficient proves that this dependence
is genuine for the selected branch.

The resulting investigation is easiest to follow by distinguishing three
objects:
\[
 \text{the Hankel sequence},\qquad
 \text{the associated algebraic curve},\qquad
 \text{a chosen generating-function branch}.
\]
The first two objects admit descriptions over $K$.  A chosen branch need not.
This distinction explains why removing radicals from the curve does not by
itself remove them from the normalized generating function.

Sections~2 and~3 construct the six-parameter extension and locate the first
fractional power.  Section~4 shows that the Hankel orbit and the associated
curve nevertheless descend to $K$.  Section~5 compares the result with Chang
and Liu's generic quadratic-algebraic Hankel construction and gives an explicit
dictionary between the two descriptions.  Section~6 studies the remaining
branch and normalization problem and records several transformations that
cannot solve it.  Section~7 states precisely what remains open.
\section{From the five-parameter construction to six parameters}
\subsection{The five-parameter input}
Let
\[
 G_{1;\lambda,m,r,\eta,\tau}(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}g_kx^k
\]
be the selected algebraic series of \cite{Scheuerle2026}.  Define
\[
 H_N(g)=\det(g_{i+j})_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},\qquad
 D_N(g)=\det(g_{i+j+1})_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}.
\]
The normalization in the preceding paper is
\begin{equation}
 (H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)=(\lambda,m,r,\eta),
 \qquad D_N=H_{N+2}.
 \label{eq:old-normalization}
\end{equation}
Its quadratic discriminant factors as
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_1(x)
 =\bigl(x\lambda^2\eta m r^2\bigr)^2
  \left((1-Jx+\tau x^2)^2-4x^3\right),
 \label{eq:old-discriminant}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 J=\frac{\lambda\eta}{mr}
   +\frac{m^2}{\lambda r}
   +\frac{r^2}{m\eta}
   +\frac{\tau mr}{\lambda\eta}.
 \label{eq:old-J}
\end{equation}
These statements are the only inputs from the five-parameter construction
needed below.

\subsection{Weighted transport}
Work over
\[
 \K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,q,\tau),\qquad q^6=\alpha,
\]
and introduce the primed parameters
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'=\lambda,\qquad m'=q^{-2}m,\qquad
 r'=q^{-6}r,\qquad \eta'=q^{-12}\eta,\qquad
 \tau'=q^{-8}\tau.
 \label{eq:parameter-scaling}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 G_{\alpha;\lambda,m,r,\eta,\tau}(x)
 :=G_{1;\lambda',m',r',\eta',\tau'}(qx).
 \label{eq:transported-G}
\end{equation}
If
\[
 G_{1;\lambda',m',r',\eta',\tau'}(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}a_kx^k,
 \qquad
 G_{\alpha;\lambda,m,r,\eta,\tau}(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}b_kx^k,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 b_k=q^ka_k.
 \label{eq:coefficient-scaling}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Determinant weights]
For every $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 H_N(b)=q^{N(N-1)}H_N(a),\qquad
 D_N(b)=q^{N^2}D_N(a).
 \label{eq:determinant-weights}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the ordinary Hankel matrix, its $(i,j)$ entry is multiplied by $q^{i+j}$.
Factoring $q^i$ from row $i$ and $q^j$ from column $j$ gives the exponent
$2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}j=N(N-1)$.  For the shifted matrix the entry is multiplied
by $q^{i+j+1}$.  The additional factor $q$ in every row contributes $N$, so
the total exponent is $N(N-1)+N=N^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The six-parameter Somos--4 recurrence}
\begin{theorem}
Let $\Ha_N=H_N(b)$.  Then
\[
 (\Ha_1,\Ha_2,\Ha_3,\Ha_4)=(\lambda,m,r,\eta)
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 \Ha_{k+4}\Ha_k
 =\alpha\Ha_{k+3}\Ha_{k+1}+\tau\Ha_{k+2}^2,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:transported-recurrence}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:determinant-weights} and
\eqref{eq:parameter-scaling} give
\[
 \Ha_1=\lambda,
 \quad \Ha_2=q^2m'=m,
 \quad \Ha_3=q^6r'=r,
 \quad \Ha_4=q^{12}\eta'=\eta.
\]
Write $H'_N=H_N(a)$.  The primed sequence satisfies
\[
 H'_{k+4}H'_k=H'_{k+3}H'_{k+1}+\tau'H'_{k+2}{}^2.
\]
The weight difference between the left-hand product and the first product on
the right is $6$, while that between the left-hand product and
$H'_{k+2}{}^2$ is $8$.  Hence
\[
 \Ha_{k+4}\Ha_k
 =q^6\Ha_{k+3}\Ha_{k+1}+q^8\tau'\Ha_{k+2}^2.
\]
Now use $q^6=\alpha$ and $q^8\tau'=\tau$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Low-order recurrence check]
The first two values forced beyond the initial tuple are
\[
 \Ha_5=\frac{\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2}{\lambda},
\qquad
 \Ha_6=\frac{\alpha\Ha_5r+\tau\eta^2}{m}.
\]
These formulas provide a direct indexing check for any coefficient expansion
of \eqref{eq:transported-G}.
\end{corollary}

\section{Why fractional powers appear}
The weighted transport solves the ordinary Hankel-determinant problem, but it does not preserve the companion normalization in its original form.  This is the point at which the stronger rationality question enters.

\subsection{The transported companion identity}
\begin{proposition}
If the primed series obeys $D_N(a)=H'_{N+2}$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Da_N:=D_N(b)=q^{-3N-2}\Ha_{N+2},
 \qquad N\geq1.
 \label{eq:graded-companion}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:determinant-weights},
\[
 D_N(b)=q^{N^2}H'_{N+2},\qquad
 \Ha_{N+2}=q^{(N+2)(N+1)}H'_{N+2}.
\]
Their exponent difference is
$N^2-(N+2)(N+1)=-3N-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A constant coefficient rescaling $b_k\mapsto cb_k$ changes the ratio
$D_N(b)/\Ha_{N+2}$ by $c^{-2}$.  It cannot remove the factor $q^{-3N}$.
Thus the literal identity $D_N=H_{N+2}$ is not invariant under this graded
transport.  Equation \eqref{eq:graded-companion}, rather than the ungraded
identity, is the companion normalization naturally inherited from the
five-parameter series.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The first coefficients and failure of descent}
For the five-parameter selected series, the companion normalization gives
$g_0=\lambda$ and $g_1=r$.  Applied to the primed parameters in
\eqref{eq:parameter-scaling}, this yields $a_0=\lambda$ and
$a_1=r'=q^{-6}r$.  Therefore \eqref{eq:coefficient-scaling} gives
\begin{equation}
 b_0=\lambda,\qquad b_1=q^{-5}r=\frac{qr}{\alpha}.
 \label{eq:first-coefficients}
\end{equation}
The next two coefficients obtained by direct expansion are
\begin{align}
 b_2&=\frac{m}{\lambda}+\frac{q^{-10}r^2}{\lambda}
     =\frac{m}{\lambda}+\frac{q^2r^2}{\alpha^2\lambda},
 \label{eq:b2}\\
 b_3&=\frac{q^5m^2}{\lambda^2r}
 +\frac{q^3\eta}{\alpha r}
 +\frac{2qmr}{\alpha\lambda^2}
 +\frac{q^3r^3}{\alpha^3\lambda^2}.
 \label{eq:b3}
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G_\alpha(x)={}&\lambda+\frac{qr}{\alpha}x
 +\left(\frac{m}{\lambda}+\frac{q^2r^2}{\alpha^2\lambda}\right)x^2\\
&+\left(\frac{q^5m^2}{\lambda^2r}
 +\frac{q^3\eta}{\alpha r}
 +\frac{2qmr}{\alpha\lambda^2}
 +\frac{q^3r^3}{\alpha^3\lambda^2}\right)x^3+O(x^4).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:series-expansion}
\end{equation}
The first coefficient also follows from \eqref{eq:graded-companion} at $N=1$:
\[
 b_1=D_1(b)=q^{-5}H_3(b)=q^{-5}r.
\]
Thus the fractional power is forced by the transported companion data rather
than caused by an insufficient simplification.

Put
\[
 K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau),\qquad
 L=K(q),\qquad q^6=\alpha.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Non-descent of the transported branch]
Regard $\alpha$ as an indeterminate.  In the generic nondegenerate case
$r\neq0$, the transported series \eqref{eq:transported-G} does not belong to
$K[[x]]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Its coefficient of $x$ is $b_1=q^{-5}r=qr/\alpha$, which is not in $K$ for
generic $\alpha$.  Equivalently, if $\sigma(q)=\zeta q$ with
$\zeta^6=1$ and $\zeta\neq1$, then
$\sigma(b_1)=\zeta^{-5}b_1=\zeta b_1\neq b_1$.
\end{proof}

Although the coefficients do not descend, the fractional powers cancel in the
ordinary Hankel determinants.  For example,
\[
 H_2(b)=b_0b_2-b_1^2
 =\lambda\left(\frac{m}{\lambda}
 +\frac{q^{-10}r^2}{\lambda}\right)-q^{-10}r^2=m.
\]
Together with the theorem above, this establishes the precise distinction:
ordinary Hankel descent holds, whereas descent of the selected transported
series fails.

\section{What is defined over the six-parameter field}
Although the selected transported series does not descend, this does not imply that the underlying recurrence or its curve requires fractional parameters.  We now separate these levels.

\subsection{Transport of the discriminant}
Separate the old invariant into
\begin{equation}
 A=\frac{\lambda\eta}{mr}+\frac{\tau mr}{\lambda\eta},
 \qquad
 B=\frac{m^2}{\lambda r}+\frac{r^2}{m\eta}.
 \label{eq:A-B}
\end{equation}
After substitution of \eqref{eq:parameter-scaling},
\begin{equation}
 J'=q^{-4}A+q^2B.
 \label{eq:J-prime}
\end{equation}
Consequently the substitution $x\mapsto qx$ gives
\begin{equation}
 qJ'=q^{-3}A+q^3B
 =\alpha^{-1/2}A+\alpha^{1/2}B.
 \label{eq:J-alpha}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Transported discriminant]
The discriminant obtained by substituting
\eqref{eq:parameter-scaling} and $x\mapsto qx$ into
\eqref{eq:old-discriminant} is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\alpha(x)
 =q^{-50}\bigl(x\lambda^2\eta m r^2\bigr)^2
 \left[
  \left(1-(q^{-3}A+q^3B)x+q^{-6}\tau x^2\right)^2
  -4q^3x^3
 \right].
 \label{eq:q-discriminant}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\alpha(x)
 =\alpha^{-25/3}\bigl(x\lambda^2\eta m r^2\bigr)^2
 \left[
  \left(1-(\alpha^{-1/2}A+\alpha^{1/2}B)x
             +\frac{\tau}{\alpha}x^2\right)^2
  -4\alpha^{1/2}x^3
 \right].
 \label{eq:alpha-discriminant}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The square prefactor has total $q$-weight
$2(1-12-2-12)=-50$.  The three terms in the residual factor transform as
$J'x\mapsto qJ'x$, $\tau'x^2\mapsto q^2\tau'x^2=q^{-6}\tau x^2$, and
$x^3\mapsto q^3x^3$.  Equations \eqref{eq:J-prime} and $q^6=\alpha$ give the
stated forms.
\end{proof}

The overall monomial factor in \eqref{eq:q-discriminant} is a square in
$\K(x)$ and does not affect the residual double cover.  It should, however,
be retained when comparing discriminants of particular polynomial
representatives, because multiplying a quadratic equation by a scalar squares
that scalar in its discriminant.

\subsection{A rational model of the curve}
Remove the square prefactor in \eqref{eq:alpha-discriminant} and put
\begin{equation}
 x=\alpha^{1/2}z.
 \label{eq:x-z}
\end{equation}
The residual equation becomes
\begin{equation}
 Y^2=\left(1-(A+\alpha B)z+\tau z^2\right)^2
      -4\alpha^2z^3.
 \label{eq:rational-quartic-z}
\end{equation}
All coefficients of \eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} belong to
$\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$.

For comparison with the quartic convention used in \cite{Scheuerle2026}, set
$X=z^{-1}$ and $Y=z^{-2}y$.  Then \eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} is birational
to
\begin{equation}
 y^2=\left(X^2-(A+\alpha B)X+\tau\right)^2-4\alpha^2X.
 \label{eq:rational-quartic-X}
\end{equation}
Thus the curve descends to the rational six-parameter field.  The coordinate
change \eqref{eq:x-z} nevertheless rescales coefficients of a formal power
series and does not preserve the normalized Hankel data.  Curve descent and
descent of the distinguished generating series are therefore different
problems.

\subsection{Formal representatives with the same Hankel determinants}
Preservation of the ordinary Hankel transform alone does not characterize the
distinguished algebraic series.  For a nondegenerate Hankel sequence define
\begin{equation}
 \beta_n=\frac{H_{n-1}H_{n+1}}{H_n^2},\qquad H_0=1.
 \label{eq:jacobi-beta}
\end{equation}
All $H_n$ generated by \eqref{eq:transported-recurrence}, and hence all
$\beta_n$, lie in $K$.  For arbitrary $a_0,a_1,\ldots\in K$, the Jacobi
fraction
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde G(x)=
 \cfrac{H_1}{1-a_0x-
  \cfrac{\beta_1x^2}{1-a_1x-
   \cfrac{\beta_2x^2}{1-a_2x-\ddots}}}
 \label{eq:jacobi-representative}
\end{equation}
has ordinary Hankel determinants $H_n$.  Thus radical-free formal-series
representatives over $K$ exist, but they need not be quadratic algebraic,
preserve the graded companion identity, or arise from the distinguished
Stieltjes splitting.

\section{Comparison with the construction of Chang and Liu}
The preceding results leave open whether a different quadratic-algebraic generating function can avoid fractional powers.  The construction of Chang and Liu is directly relevant because it realizes a generic bilateral Somos--4 orbit by Hankel determinants.  We therefore compare the two descriptions explicitly before considering further repairs.

\subsection{The shifted Somos--4 orbit}
Chang and Liu \cite[Sections~3.3--4.2]{ChangLiu2026} treat the generic bilateral Somos--4 initial-value problem by Hankel determinants of quadratic orthogonal pairs. Their normalized recurrence is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{n+2}\sigma_{n-2}=a\sigma_{n+1}\sigma_{n-1}+b\sigma_n^2,
 \qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
 \label{eq:CL-recurrence}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{-1}=x$, $\sigma_0=1$, $\sigma_1=y$, and $\sigma_2=z$.
Our freely prescribed determinants fit this normalization after the shift
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_n=\frac{H_{n+2}}m,
 \qquad
 (x,y,z;a,b)=\left(\frac\lambda m,\frac r m,\frac\eta m;\alpha,\tau\right).
 \label{eq:CL-dictionary}
\end{equation}
This uses all six parameters and avoids the constraint
$\eta=\alpha\lambda r+\tau m^2$ that would follow from imposing our recurrence at $N=0$ with $H_0=1$.

Write $u^2=\alpha$ and define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal N&=\tau r^2m+\lambda^2\eta^2+\alpha\eta m^3+\alpha\lambda r^3,
 \label{eq:CL-Nplus}\\
 \mathcal N_0&=\tau r^2m-\lambda^2\eta^2+\alpha\eta m^3+\alpha\lambda r^3.
 \label{eq:CL-Nminus}
\end{align}
Under \eqref{eq:CL-dictionary}, the Chang--Liu parameters include
\begin{align}
 d_0&=\frac{\lambda r}{m^2},&u_0&=\frac{2r}{m},\\
 \frac vu&=\frac{\mathcal N}{2\alpha\lambda r\eta m},&
 uv&=\frac{\mathcal N}{2\lambda r\eta m},\\
 \frac{v_0}{u}&=\frac{\mathcal N_0}{2\alpha\lambda r\eta m},&
 uv_0&=\frac{\mathcal N_0}{2\lambda r\eta m},
 \label{eq:CL-parameters}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 f=\frac{\tau}{\alpha}-
 \frac{\mathcal N^2}{4\alpha\lambda^2r^2\eta^2m^2}.
 \label{eq:CL-f}
\end{equation}
The sign difference between $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal N_0$ is essential because $v_0$ enters the distinguished moment branch.
The scaling $X=uZ$ turns their curve into
\begin{equation}
 Y^2=(\alpha Z^2+f)^2+4\alpha Z-4uv,
 \label{eq:CL-descended-curve}
\end{equation}
which is defined over $K$. This is curve-level descent, not coefficientwise descent of a selected moment branch.

\subsection{Translation to Jacobi data}
Use the local Jacobi convention
\begin{equation}
 F(t)=\cfrac{\lambda}{1-A_0t-
 \cfrac{B_1t^2}{1-A_1t-
 \cfrac{B_2t^2}{1-A_2t-
 \cfrac{B_3t^2}{1-A_3t-B_4t^2M_4(t)}}}},
 \qquad M_4(0)=1.
 \label{eq:local-jacobi-convention}
\end{equation}
The product formula
\begin{equation}
 H_N=\lambda^N\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}B_j^{N-j}
 \label{eq:J-Hankel-product}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{align}
 B_1&=\frac{m}{\lambda^2},&
 B_2&=\frac{\lambda r}{m^2},&
 B_3&=\frac{m\eta}{r^2},&
 B_4&=\frac{r(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)}{\lambda\eta^2}.
 \label{eq:first-B}
\end{align}
The companion identities through $D_3=H_5$ force
\begin{align}
 A_0&=\frac r\lambda,&
 A_1&=\frac{\eta\lambda^2+m^2}{\lambda mr},&
 A_2&=\frac{\alpha\eta m^3+\lambda r^3+\tau m^2r^2}{\eta\lambda mr}.
 \label{eq:first-A}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Fourth shifted determinant]
For the convention \eqref{eq:local-jacobi-convention},
\begin{equation}
 D_4=\lambda^4B_1^3B_2^2B_3
 \left(A_0A_1A_2A_3-A_0A_1B_3-A_0A_3B_2-A_2A_3B_1+B_1B_3\right).
 \label{eq:generic-D4}
\end{equation}
After substitution of \eqref{eq:first-B} and \eqref{eq:first-A}, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
 D_4=\frac{\eta}{\lambda r^2}
 \left[A_3r(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)-\eta^2\lambda\right].
 \label{eq:D4-reduced}
\end{equation}
Consequently $D_4=H_6$, where
\[
 H_6=\frac{\alpha^2\eta mr+\alpha\tau r^3+\tau\eta^2\lambda}{\lambda m},
\]
uniquely forces
\begin{equation}
 A_3=
 \frac{\alpha^2\eta mr^3+\alpha\tau r^5+\eta^3\lambda m+\tau\eta^2\lambda r^2}
 {\eta mr(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)}.
 \label{eq:A3}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:generic-D4} follows by direct expansion of the shifted $4\times4$ Hankel determinant in Jacobi parameters.  Substitution and collection of the terms linear in $A_3$ gives \eqref{eq:D4-reduced}.  Solving \eqref{eq:D4-reduced} for $A_3$ after inserting the displayed value of $H_6$ gives \eqref{eq:A3}.
\end{proof}
An equivalent useful form is
\[
 A_3=\frac{\alpha r^2}{\eta m}+
 \frac{\eta\lambda(\eta m+\tau r^2)}{mr(\alpha\eta m+\tau r^2)}.
\]
No $B_4$ occurs in $D_4$: the fourth ordinary Hankel step determines $B_4$, while the fourth shifted step determines $A_3$, without imposing any extra relation among the six parameters.

Chang and Liu use
\[
 d_n=\frac{\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n+1}}{\sigma_n^2}.
\]
Substitution of \eqref{eq:CL-dictionary} gives the exact all-order interface
\begin{equation}
 d_n=\frac{H_{n+1}H_{n+3}}{H_{n+2}^2}=B_{n+2}.
 \label{eq:d-B}
\end{equation}
Thus $d_0=B_2$, $d_1=B_3$, and $d_2=B_4$.  At the subdiagonal level only $B_1$ precedes the Chang--Liu tail; the diagonal and moment normalizations remain separate issues.

\section{What the comparison resolves}
The comparison confirms the same basic distinction found for the weighted transport: the orbit and the curve may descend even when one distinguished moment branch does not.

\subsection{Even and odd parts of the Chang--Liu branch}
Let
\[
 S(t;u)=\sum_{n\ge0}s_n(u)t^n,\qquad u^2=\alpha,
\]
be the distinguished positive Chang--Liu moment branch.  The two choices of square root satisfy
\begin{equation}
 S(t;-u)=S(-t;u),\qquad s_n(-u)=(-1)^ns_n(u).
 \label{eq:galois-parity}
\end{equation}
Hence the two branches have equal ordinary Hankel determinants, but a single branch is generically not fixed by $u\mapsto-u$ and therefore does not descend coefficientwise to $K[[t]]$.
Writing
\[
 S(t;u)=E(t^2;u)+tO(t^2;u)
\]
gives $E(z;u)\in K[[z]]$ and $O(z;u)/u\in K[[z]]$, whereas $O$ is generically anti-invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Parity-rescaling obstruction]
Let $r_{2k}=a s_{2k}$ and $r_{2k+1}=b s_{2k+1}$.  If the generic identity $H_3(r)=\kappa H_3(s)$ holds coefficientwise, then $a^3=\kappa$, $ab^2=\kappa$, and therefore $b^2=a^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Compare the coefficients of the independent monomials in the two generic $3\times3$ Hankel determinants.  The pure even contribution has weight $a^3$, while the terms containing two odd moments have weight $ab^2$.
\end{proof}
In particular, the descended recombination
$R(t)=E(t^2;u)+tO(t^2;u)/u$ fails already at order two:
\[
 H_2(R)-H_2(S)=\left(1-\frac1\alpha\right)s_1^2.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Geometric-scaling obstruction]
No generic scaling $r_n=cd^ns_n$ converts the normalized Chang--Liu determinants $H_N(s)=H_{N+2}/m$ into $H_N(r)=H_{N+2}$ for all $N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Such a scaling gives $H_N(r)=c^Nd^{N(N-1)}H_N(s)$, so one would need $c^Nd^{N(N-1)}=m$.  The cases $N=1,2$ force $c=m$ and $d^2=m^{-1}$, while $N=3$ then gives $c^3d^6=1$, not $m$, generically.
\end{proof}
Finally, the Galois-equivariant obstruction proved above applies to a finite Jacobi prefix over $K$: as a generically invertible linear-fractional transformation in its tail, it cannot turn one non-descending Chang--Liu branch into a descending one.  This excludes the direct prefix strategy, but not every possible quadratic-algebraic representative.

\subsection{Why base-field transforms cannot repair a branch}
The following observation excludes a broad class of attempted rationalizations.
\begin{proposition}[Galois-equivariant obstruction]
Let $T:L[[x]]\to L[[x]]$ be injective and commute with every
$K$-automorphism of $L$.  If $T(G)\in K[[x]]$, then $G\in K[[x]]$.
If $T$ is invertible, the two conditions are equivalent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$,
$T(\sigma G)=\sigma T(G)=T(G)$.  Injectivity gives $\sigma G=G$, so $G$ is
fixed by the Galois action and belongs to $K[[x]]$.
\end{proof}

For $c\in K$, the binomial transform
\[
 B_c(G)(x)=\frac{1}{1-cx}G\!\left(\frac{x}{1-cx}\right)
\]
is defined over $K$, commutes with the Galois action, and is invertible with
inverse $B_{-c}$.  Likewise, the invert transform
\[
 I_c(G)(x)=\frac{G(x)}{1-cxG(x)}
\]
is defined over $K$, Galois-equivariant, and invertible with inverse $I_{-c}$.
Consequently neither transform can turn the non-descending transported branch
into a series in $K[[x]]$.  This conclusion concerns these transforms over the
base field; it does not exclude a new algebraic construction with different
companion data.

\section{Remaining possibilities}
The established facts now separate three levels cleanly: the Chang--Liu curve descends; its ordinary Hankel sequence descends; an individual distinguished moment branch does not descend generically.  The rational Jacobi calculation extends through $A_3$ and $B_4$, and the subdiagonal interface is exactly $d_n=B_{n+2}$.

The remaining constructive possibility should therefore be sought in the invariant pair
\[
 E(z),\qquad R(z):=O(z)/u,
\]
rather than by attaching an injective $K$-rational prefix to one raw branch.  Starting from the quadratic equation for $S(t;u)$, one should substitute
\[
 S(t;u)=E(t^2)+utR(t^2)
\]
and separate invariant and anti-invariant parts.  This yields coupled algebraic equations over $K$ for $E$ and $R$.  A non-diagonal rational construction from this pair can then be tested against
\[
 f_0=\lambda,\qquad f_1=r,\qquad f_2=\frac{r^2+m}{\lambda}
\]
and the determinant conditions through $D_4=H_6$.

Thus no general nonexistence claim is made.  What is excluded is the narrower class consisting of parity rescalings, constant mass and argument scalings, alternating signs, and injective finite prefixes over $K$ applied to a single distinguished Chang--Liu branch.  A constructive all-order theorem, or a precise nonexistence theorem for a broader class, remains open.

\section{Conclusion}
We began with the five-parameter generating function of
\cite{Scheuerle2026} and asked whether it extends to the general
two-coefficient Somos--4 recurrence.  The weighted substitution
\eqref{eq:transported-G} gives an exact extension for arbitrary
$(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$.  It preserves the prescribed ordinary
Hankel determinants and yields \eqref{eq:transported-recurrence}.  Thus the
six-parameter Hankel problem is solved at the level of the recurrence.

The same construction reveals a second and more delicate problem.  Its
companion determinants satisfy the graded identity
\eqref{eq:graded-companion}, and the coefficient $b_1=q^{-5}r$ shows that the
selected branch does not generically descend to
$K=\Q(\lambda,m,r,\eta,\alpha,\tau)$.  This is not merely an unsimplified
formula.  On the other hand, the rational quartic
\eqref{eq:rational-quartic-z} shows that the associated curve does descend.
The Hankel sequence, the curve, and a distinguished generating-function branch
must therefore be treated as separate objects.

The comparison with Chang and Liu makes this separation explicit in a second
construction.  The shifted dictionary \eqref{eq:CL-dictionary} identifies
their generic bilateral orbit with ours, and the all-order relation
$d_n=B_{n+2}$ identifies the subdiagonal Jacobi data.  The rational restoration
through $A_3$ and $B_4$ shows that the prescribed companion data are consistent
at the computed levels.  Nevertheless, the parity relation
\eqref{eq:galois-parity} shows that an individual distinguished moment branch
is generically non-descending.

The remaining question is consequently narrower than the original one.  It is
unknown whether another quadratic-algebraic representative can simultaneously
have coefficients in $K$, realize the prescribed six-parameter Hankel orbit,
and satisfy the ungraded identity $D_N=H_{N+2}$.  Parity rescaling, geometric
scaling, and injective finite prefixes over $K$ cannot achieve all three goals.
These obstructions do not constitute a general nonexistence theorem.  A
non-diagonal construction based on the invariant pair $(E,O/u)$ remains a
concrete possibility for further investigation.
